## Containers, pools, and logging

Two fixed-size node pools use the same free-list pattern. Pool A grows 0x178-byte nodes (0x896d40), pops at 0x896e80, returns nodes at 0x896ef0, and removes a fully free block at 0x896e20. The pool counter at +0x0c and per-block counter at +0x18 count free nodes: growth adds capacity, pop subtracts one, push adds one, and a block is releasable when its free count reaches capacity. Its pool subobject is at List+0x10; RTTI binds adjustor thunks 0x896f60/0x896f70 to `SoeUtil::List<Logging::LoggingApi::QueueEntry,128>` slots 2/3, and their raw bytes add that offset before jumping to pop/push. The block registry uses the address-tree erase at 0x896b90.

Pool B uses 0x8e0-byte nodes for the LoggingFile hash map (growth 0x89a380, free-block 0x89a450, pop/push 0x89a4b0/0x89a520). Its pool lives at map+0x8014, which the thunks 0x89a590/0x89a5a0 add before transferring to the shared HashList implementation. The tree helpers 0x899c80-0x89a1d0 cover successor/predecessor, relink/rotations, lower-bound, fixups, insert, and erase. Address 0x89a620 is a five-byte direct jump to 0x899b50, not a second copy of that destructor body.

`Logging::LoggingApi` initializes a 128-slot rate tracker and queue/list members at 0x897050; its destructor is 0x8973e0. The tracker stores sums/counts per bucket, rolling totals at +0x408/+0x40c, and its window at +0x410 (constructor 0x897c70, reset 0x897910, advance 0x897d00, sample 0x897df0, queries 0x897e50/0x897e80/0x897eb0). The file manager at 0x89a8b0 owns the date strings, hash/list of files, and two trackers. Log files are initialized at 0x898e20; 0x8989c0 performs buffered writes and failover, 0x899380 formats/queues lines, and 0x898f50/0x898ff0 flush one/all files. Date rotation is 0x899050; named-channel lookup/write is 0x899950.

The decompiler export for 0x899220 stops at `_free`, but the bytes after its exported range continue cleanup and return. Its record therefore does not claim a non-returning tail-free. The generic tree cleanup at 0x8986d0 is not bound to the 8192-bucket map because this function has no class anchor. The erase fixup at 0x899fa0 performs sibling/parent recoloring and rotations, but has no final root-color store.

## Packet families and direction

ClientGateway wrapper constructors write opcode 5 for client-packet tunnels (0x89adc0) and 6 for client-world tunnels (0x89ae70). Client-side setup paths build these wrappers around serialized payloads. This identifies the envelope format; it does not reveal the original gateway's dispatch implementation.

The ordinary BasePacket constructors show outbound setup/notification paths for opcode 10 (finished loading, 0x89b000), opcode 16 (logout, 0x89b120), and opcode 169 (initialization details, 0x89bfa0). Opcode 169 is constructed and serialized in the successful character-data path 0x938a50 via 0x929d60. Opcode 10 is constructed from the world-ready path 0x936d00. Opcode 16 is constructed and wrapped in the disconnect path 0x94a410. By contrast, opcode 117 (0x89b510) and opcode 142 (0x89bb60) have no demonstrated transport direction: their visible callers construct them, but do not show that same instance reaching a send or receive path.

The inbound packet factory/dispatcher at 0x9471a0 constructs and parses the following opcode families in this shard: 12 self/profile data (0x89b240; Research/Pieces/043 records observed profile fields), 73 shutdown notice, 86 world-display fields, 89 area, 100 membership activation, 103 system message (color lookup 0x89b950), 104 POI change, 144 game settings, 145/199/201 trial-upgrade prompts, 157 user-age values, 171 coin-spin availability, 177 FlashTimer, 189 subscription flags, 190 CAIS fields, and 208 composite-effect data. The constructor defaults at 0x89c2b0 and 0x89c380 are overwritten by parsing; they are not evidence of server-sent defaults. In particular, the constructor for opcode 177 clears a byte at +8 and a full dword at +0x0c.

The client-update family is opcode 38 with a sub-opcode at +8. Base construction is at 0x89d120/0x89d180 and parsing/dispatch is at 0x9392c0. Catalogue-backed classes in this shard map to 38/1 Hitpoints, 38/2 ItemAdd, 38/4 ItemDelete, 38/6 UnequipSlot, 38/8 CollectionStart, and 38/13 Mana. The RTTI-named Storage constructor at 0x89d5e0 supplies sub-opcode 31 and two dwords, but no matching class entry was found in the packet catalogue. `wire` records for subclasses use their catalogue class names where available; the CollectionStart catalogue entry identifies the complete 38/8 pair even though its local constructor only passes literal 8 to the base constructor. Team notes 038, 044, and 056 are consistent with the family frame and the separate collection/effect sub-opcodes they discuss.

These are hypotheses about a lost server contract, not recovered server behavior. The client dispatcher establishes which messages it accepts and what client code runs; opcode names and handler behavior in Sanctuary/FreeRealms-Legacy establish only those reconstructions. The community PacketReaderExtensions files map opcode 16 to `PacketClientLogout`, 73 to `PacketClientServerShuttingDown`, and 169 to `PacketClientInitializationDetails` (both pinned copies: `../references/Sanctuary/src/Sanctuary.Packet.Common/Extensions/PacketReaderExtensions.cs:87,643,1119` and `../references/FreeRealms-Legacy/src/Sanctuary.Packet.Common/Extensions/PacketReaderExtensions.cs:87,643,1119`). These rows correct the earlier draft's claim that 16 and 73 were known only from RTTI; they do not establish how the original server handled them. FreeRealms-Legacy describes itself as an emulator built from scratch in its [repository README](https://github.com/raisingkaines/FreeRealms-Legacy), so its logic remains third-party evidence.
