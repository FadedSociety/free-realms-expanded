## Threat note (secure-code-guardian)

Hostile client capabilities on family 40/sub 4 and why they are constrained:

- **Forged body identities.** The 40/4 body carries two GUIDs, two name
  records, a group id, an accept byte and five opaque dwords
  (`src/bin/osfr-gateway.rs:2899-2911`). The parser consumes and discards every
  identity field; only the accept byte reaches the service. The actor is always
  the authenticated `self.character_guid` (`src/bin/osfr-gateway.rs:2166-2170`),
  so a client cannot accept on another character's behalf or select a group.
- **Stale invitation.** A character that joined another party after the invite
  arrived still has the pending invite keyed by its GUID. The guard
  (`src/social_lists.rs:784-790`) refuses with 40/5 result 1 before consuming
  the invite or touching either roster, so the request cannot produce
  overlapping membership or move the character out of the party it joined.
- **Replay.** A repeated 40/4 has no pending invite and gets 40/5 result 3
  without changing state (`src/social_lists.rs:767-769`). A first accept or
  decline removes the invite.
- **Forged decline.** Decline only removes the target's own pending invite and
  notifies the existing group (`src/social_lists.rs:792-800`); it cannot remove
  membership or grant anything.
- **Data and economy.** 40/4 carries no quantity, item, coin or reward. The
  refusal message is a fixed 6-byte 40/5 packet built by
  `encode_group_result`; no save file, item, coin or cooldown is written.
- **Trust boundary.** The service trusts only the session GUID and its own
  pending-invite map. Client-supplied names/GUIDs are parsed only because the
  fixed body length must be consumed; mismatch is ignored, not merged.
- Existing exposure (not introduced here): `SocialService` has no per-account
  action budget for group requests, so repeated 40/1 or 40/4 packets are limited
  only by the transport/session limits, not by an application budget. The invite
  TTL bounds how long a pending invite reserves capacity, not the request rate.
  Adding a group-specific budget is a security decision for the lead.
