## Findings and what changed

1. **BLOCKER: the walk check no longer sampled the step endpoint.**
   - `src/pet_follow.rs` `advance` checked only the `sample_m` lookahead, so the 0.25-0.5 m point the
     pet actually moves to was tested only by the incidental 0.5 m path samples. A hole or a dip
     narrower than `sample_m` at that point was stepped onto (the review's two concrete failures).
   - The single `walkable(from, lookahead)` call is now two, evaluated endpoint first:
     `walkable(from, [x, y, z])` (the point this step lands on) and
     `walkable(from, [check_x, y, check_z])` (the lookahead). This is exactly the pre-F850
     step-endpoint check restored next to the lookahead F850 added: it refuses unknown ground at the
     endpoint (`gridded && stepped.is_none()`) and a rise/drop over `step_up_m`/`step_down_m` at the
     endpoint, including a dip between the 0.5 m samples.
   - The `checked` gate is unchanged, so a zone with no height grid at all still keeps T455's
     owner-height rule; the endpoint check only runs when some ground exists (or the zone is
     gridded).
   - Regression tests (both fail on the F850 code, see the canary under Gates):
     - `a_fifty_millisecond_step_does_not_walk_into_a_narrow_dip` (`tests/pet_navigation.rs`): a
       0.25 m height grid with one node 4 m down exactly at the pet's first 20 Hz step endpoint,
       while the 0.5 m lookahead is back on flat ground. It drives the real request path:
       `Gameplay::handle_in_world` accepts the 53/26 click, then `Gameplay::pet_tick` runs the
       production walk. It asserts the pet never drops below y = 0 and never moves from its spawn
       until the `warp_far_ms` warp, that the warp waits `warp_far_ms` from the first refused step,
       and that it lands beside the owner.
     - `a_step_endpoint_over_a_hole_is_refused` (`tests/pet_navigation.rs`): the pure production
       `pet_follow::step` with ground missing only at the step endpoint (the same shape cannot be
       built with the shared `terrain::surface` helper without an annulus, because a hole shows as
       `None` only when all four nodes of a cell are empty). The pet must not move and the step
       sends nothing.
   - Pace: the review's "0.1 m step endpoint" is the F850-era 2 m/s `min_speed` pace. T827 (landed
     after F850, before this fix) paces the walk at `pets.json` `movement_speed` = 5.0 m/s, so the
     production step is 0.25 m; the dip regression uses the shipped pace. The rule is the same at
     either pace. The stale "2 m/s" claim in [[T490]] and the "0.1 m" test comment are corrected.
2. **MINOR: the "at most 81 samples" claim.** Corrected in [[F850]] and [[T490]] (checklist 19) to
   `ceil(path / sample_m) + 1` ground samples: 81 on a 40 m path at the shipped 0.5 m spacing, 401
   at the `FollowConfig::valid` minimum of 0.1 m, and still capped by `MAX_WALK_SAMPLES` (4096).
   The per-tick cost is now up to 4 ground calls (endpoint pair plus lookahead pair) on a moving pet.
3. **Note claims corrected.** [[F850]]'s behaviour summary and [[T490]]'s follow summary now say the
   step endpoint is checked as well as the lookahead; the old "never steps over a hole in a gridded
   zone" claim is corrected with an F858 marker in both notes; the per-tick and 53/26 sample counts
   and the click pace are corrected as above.

## Behaviour summary

- A follow step or a clicked-destination step now checks two points before the pose moves: the point
  this step lands on (the restored endpoint check) and, when the target is reached within one step,
  one `sample_m` beyond it. Unknown ground or a rise over `step_up_m` / drop over `step_down_m`
  between two of those checks refuses the step and starts the existing no-progress clock; after
  `warp_far_ms` the pet warps beside the owner. A gridless zone keeps the T455 owner-height rule.
- A pet can no longer step onto a hole, or down a dip, that is narrower than `sample_m` and sits at
  the next step endpoint, which was the review's concrete failure.

## Threat note (secure-code-guardian)

This change touches the movement of a server-owned actor driven by a client click; no parser, save,
coin, item or reward is touched. What a hostile client could send, and why it is refused:

- **53/26 with any body.** The decoder is unchanged: it checks the 17-byte body before indexing,
  requires the one flag value the client writes (0), and rejects non-finite or `|v| > 1e6` floats
  with `Err`, so nothing panics and no state is written.
- **A well-formed click near a hole/dip.** The click gates are unchanged; if the click is accepted
  the pet walks toward it, and the walk now refuses a step whose endpoint has no ground or drops more
  than `step_down_m`, so a hostile click cannot put the pet on unknown ground or below the
  heightfield. The refusal changes no position, sends no frame, and consumes nothing; after
  `warp_far_ms` the pet warps beside the owner, which is the existing exit for a blocked walk.
- **The pet's height.** It comes only from the heightfield (`ground(x, z)`), never from the
  client's y; the endpoint check reads the same closure, so a forged y still cannot place the pet.
- **Cost.** Per moving pet tick the walk now evaluates the closure at most 4 times (endpoint pair
  plus lookahead pair; one `from` lookup is duplicated) with no allocation and no clone, and a
  settled pet still returns before sampling. The click-accept path's existing walk and its
  `MAX_WALK_SAMPLES` 4096 cap are unchanged.
- No new state, save, broadcast, secret or path is involved; the reply and frames are unchanged.

## Checklist

1. **A refusal changes nothing.** The new refusal is `Advance::Blocked` before `p.position` is
   assigned; it starts only the pre-existing no-progress clock (`blocked_since_ms`) whose exit is the
   warp. `a_step_endpoint_over_a_hole_is_refused` asserts the pose is exactly unchanged and no frame
   is sent; `a_fifty_millisecond_step_does_not_walk_into_a_narrow_dip` asserts the pose stays at the
   spawn for every tick until the warp, and the warp waits exactly `warp_far_ms` from the first
   refusal (so the clock is not charged early). Canary broke production and failed both.
2. **Rollback covers all state.** No transaction and no new state; the check reads the height
   closure and writes nothing before the decision. The pose is the only state and is left untouched
   on refusal.
3. **Apply deltas.** Nothing is restored from an earlier snapshot: the step is computed from the
   live pose and live owner position, and a refusal simply does not assign the new pose. The test
   asserts the live spawn pose, not a copy.
4. **Busy rules are mutual.** No new activity or gate; this is the existing pet follow/walk step, and
   the `occupation` table's pets gates are unchanged.
5. **Every entry path is gated.** `advance` is called only by `step`; `walkable` is called only by
   `advance`, `step` (tests) and the click gate `follow_location`. The owner tick path
   (`follow_pet` -> `pet_tick`/`on_location`) and the clicked path (`handle_pets` ->
   `follow_location` -> `go_to` -> `step`) both route through `advance`, so neither can skip the
   endpoint rule. No parallel handler was added.
6. **Reachable in production, tested through it.** The dip regression drives
   `Gameplay::handle_in_world` (the real 53/26 route) and `Gameplay::pet_tick` with a real `Terrain`;
   the hole regression drives the production pure function `pet_follow::step` that both paths call
   (the existing `tests/pet_follow.rs` suite's own entry point), because a sub-`sample_m` terrain
   hole needs a hand-built annulus grid rather than the shared `surface` helper.
7. **Survives reconnect.** Nothing new is persisted; the pose, go target and blocked clock are
   session-only (unchanged), and `reset_session` still drops the active pet.
8. **Every exit restores.** The blocked walk's exit is the existing warp after `warp_far_ms`; the dip
   test asserts the warp frame and that the landing is beside the owner, and the hole test's shape is
   also covered by `a_pet_over_a_hole_in_a_gridded_zone_does_not_walk` (warp asserted).
9. **Economy safety.** No coin, item, XP or reward is touched; no client-reported result is read; the
   change can only refuse a movement, never grant.
10. **Wire format matches the client reader.** No wire change. The 53/26 body and the 20-byte reply
    are the F850 ones (reader `00b578d0`/`sub_8E2410`); the dip test uses the existing `can_go`
    builder and asserts the reply packet, and `00b5fcf0` was re-read this pass for the sender gates
    (see Client evidence).
11. **Unique ids.** None added; the pet actor guid and owner position are unchanged.
12. **Others see changes.** A refused step sends nothing (asserted); every accepted move still goes
    through `PetService::follow_owner`'s private+shared push, unchanged.
13. **Content works end to end.** The dip test asserts the click is accepted (can-go 1), the pet
    never enters the dip, and the final state after the exit is beside the owner; values come from

    the -4 m dip are test fixtures.
15. **Canaries break production code.** The canary removed the endpoint check from `advance` (not

17. **One player's error never stops the server.** `advance` returns an enum and never panics; the

18. **A type's rules live on the type.** No new field or type. `advance`'s doc now states that the

## Open (R) and gaps

- Not driven by a native client. The pet walk and the can-go flag remain the native check listed in
  [[F850]] (summon, click 8-40 m, watch the reply 1 and the walk; click a cliff and watch 0). The
  endpoint rule makes the server stricter, so a native run should show the pet refusing and warping
  where it previously stepped down.
- The click-acceptance path still samples its straight line at 0.5 m, so a click whose path crosses
  a dip narrower than `sample_m` is accepted (can-go 1); the walk then refuses the step and warps
  after `warp_far_ms`. That is the T490 rule (reachability of the destination), not a new gap, and
  the walk check now guards the movement itself.
- `step_up_m`/`step_down_m`/`sample_m` remain (R) (OSFR PR #152 for the first three); the owner's
  fourth float remains unreadable server-side and is taken as 0 (R) — unchanged from F850.
