## Systems and relationships

- `0x00b8a640` dispatches reward base packets by subtype. It queues subtype-1 bundle events, routes subtype-2 items to notification handling, adds subtype-6 deferred stars/XP and coins to cached banks, and clears those caches while presenting subtype-7 granted-bank totals. Its `RewardPatternOne` and callback strings anchor the item and deferred-bank paths. The existing [Reward Toast and Deferred Bank Findings](../Research/Reward%20Toast%20and%20Deferred%20Bank%20Findings.md) note independently cites this address and distinguishes display-bank updates from wallet/profile rewards.
- `0x00b8acb0`, `0x00b8ad10`, and `0x00b8add0` implement the `BaseQuickChatPacket` base: opcode `0x43` (decimal 67), subtype initialization, vtable restoration, and scalar deleting destruction. `0x00b8ae40` constructs the `QuickChatSendDataPacket` subtype-1 packet. The packet catalogue independently lists opcode 67, subtype 1 as `QuickChatSendDataPacket`; this is wire-format evidence, not original server logic.
- `0x00b8af00` maps the quick-chat internal data-source columns (`Id`, parent/text/animation/icon/item fields, favorite/recent flags and index). `0x00b8afc0` maps a two-column schema (`Id`, `CategoryName`). `0x00b8c440`, `0x00b8c470`, and `0x00b8c4a0` mutate row flags/order values; `0x00b8f1e0` reads the `Emotes`/`Favorites` definition and marks matching rows. These establish client-side quick-chat data preparation, not authoritative chat behavior.
- RTTI vtables around `0x00b8b380`–`0x00b906a0` identify a `SoeGems::TextIndex<int>` cluster: weighted provider/processing nodes, inverted-instance arrays, weighted text/instance lists, and hash maps. Helpers implement tree rotations/rebalancing, bucket growth/rehashing, list insertion/removal, array allocation and destruction. In particular `0x00b8e2a0` sums provider sizes and `0x00b8e2c0` merges two provider streams. `0x00b8f500` applies alphanumeric normalization and uppercasing. These are client-side indexing/container operations; they do not prove any game-world search rule.

## Wire and server-contract limits

The reward family is packet catalogue opcode 50 (`RewardBasePacket`) with byte subtypes 1, 2, 6 and 7. The native dispatch at `0x00b8a640` is a receive-side consumer. Subtype 6 adds deltas to the client's cached bank totals and calls UI setters with accumulated totals; subtype 7 clears those native caches and presents amounts from its own decoded body. Those observations constrain how a compatible server packet must be shaped, but do not establish original server grant, membership, persistence, replay-protection, or economy rules. The third-party Sanctuary and FreeRealms-Legacy packet names are wire-schema references only.

Quick-chat opcode 67/subtype 1 is represented by `QuickChatSendDataPacket` at `0x00b8ae40`. This constructor demonstrates packet construction and the opcode-bearing base is at `0x00b8acb0`. No server-side interpretation or accepted behavior follows from these client constructors.

## Contradictions and open questions

- The reward note documents normal bundle reward notifications separately from non-member deferred-bank presentation. Keep subtype-6/7 bank display separate from wallet/profile reward mutations; this shard contains no evidence that these handlers commit server-side grants.
- The text-index cluster contains many allocation, array, list, and tree helpers whose exact template ownership is visible only where a specific RTTI vtable is recovered. Generic helpers without a direct class anchor remain `unknown` in the JSONL rather than receiving a class by address adjacency.
- `0x00b8d080` has traversal-like parent/child reads but no recovered return value. Its intended tree-step result is unresolved.
- `0x00b8e2c0` merges child provider output using four-dword records and a float helper; the semantic meaning and tie policy of those values need data-flow into callers.
- The first word in the subtype-7 star rows and subtype-6 first body word remain unresolved in the cited reward note. No server inference should assign them meaning.


The short bodies at `0x00b8afb0`, `0x00b903d0`, and `0x00b903e0` are adjustor thunks: they adjust ECX and jump to another address. They are not ordinary call wrappers or the destination implementations. Several unanchored container helpers remain `unknown` by name despite their mechanical actions being recorded; their owning specialization is unresolved.
