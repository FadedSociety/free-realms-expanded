## 1. The on-foot controller stack (00b006d0 .. 00b02880)

`ControllerRegular` is the player's regular character controller. Its constructor `00b00ac0` installs
`ControllerRegular::vftable`, seeds its state and then caches seventeen movement tunings into the file
globals `DAT_01be8f80..DAT_01be8fe4`; four of them come straight out of the actor's property lists at
`actor+0x1b98` (id `0x2b5fbee0`), `actor+0xbf0` (`0x37a76af6`), `actor+0x1114` (`0xc21cdc3f`) and
`actor+0x568` (`0x1e259154`), the rest from `FUN_00706570` property-table lookups with a `0.0` fallback.
Those same globals are read every frame by the movement code, which is what ties the group together:

* `00b006d0` is the collision step: horizontal displacement, a step height added along the up axis, a
  convex sweep through `FUN_00fd4c20` (Bullet convex callback, mask `0x80000143`), a downward ray probe and
  a step-up retry through `FUN_00fd4290`, with `1 - hitFraction` fed back into the next of up to three
  iterations.
* `00b01040` splits one frame displacement into sub-steps and records each accepted position.
* `00b01250` (vtable slot 51) is the per-frame update; `00b02880` (slot 50) is the input dispatcher that
  switches on the queued input action ids (0x13-0x1a action bar through `FUN_008a7eb0`, 0x1c-0x21 dock
  toggles through `FUN_009797a0` with the `HotKeyEvents:Toggle*` names, 0x27 tab targeting through
  `FUN_00a2ac20`, 0x2d closest interaction through `FUN_0093bff0`). Two vault notes already cite
  00b02880: `Research/Native Combat Controls.md` and `Research/Pieces/053 Aggro and leash.md`.
* The sweep-position buffer is a `SoeUtil::Array<SoeUtil::Math::Vector<float,4>,4,1>`: ctor `00b01180`,
  destructor body `00b009b0`, scalar deleting dtor `00b011e0`, reserve hook `00b00a40` (inline capacity 4,
  tail jump to the heap path) and the slot-3 body `00b02790`, which exchanges two arrays element-wise
  through a stack temporary because the storage is inline. `00b00a70` is the sibling push for a
  heap-allocated Vector4 array.

`ControllerPreviewPet` (00b035c0 ctor, 00b03550 override, 00b036b0 deleting dtor) is the preview variant:
its slot-53 override brackets the shared base update `FUN_0094d3b0` with a save/restore of the three
dwords at +0x164..+0x16c. Note that this hierarchy keeps its scalar deleting destructor in **slot 7**,
not slot 0 (ControllerRoot::vftable[7] = `FUN_009510b0` has the identical body), so slot 0 in these
vtables is a type-id getter (00b03530 returns 0x15, 00b03540 returns 1).

## 3. Mount packet family 168 - the decoding side, and the header layout

Eight body readers in this shard (`00b04c80`, `00b04dc0`, `00b04e70`, `00b04ee0`, `00b05000`,
`00b051b0`, `00b05220`, plus the container insert `00b050d0`) are called from small decoder wrappers in
shard S0333 (`00b052f0`, `00b05360`, `00b053d0`, `00b05440`, `00b054b0`, `00b05520`, `00b05590`,
`00b05600`), which are dispatched from the mount handler `00b06100` together with the constructors and
destructors listed above.

All of them start with `00b04c20`, which reads a **u16 into packet+4 and then a u8 into packet+8**.
Those are exactly the slots the family constructor fills with `0xa8` and the sub-opcode, so the mount
payload opens with a 16-bit family opcode followed by the one-byte sub-opcode. This is a decodable wire
detail that the community serializers do not spell out.

Body shapes (each reader also validates length, zero-fills, sets reader error bytes and clamps the
cursor):

| reader | payload after the header | caller wrapper |
|---|---|---|
| `00b04c80` | guid, guid, 4 x i32, f32 (NaN-checked) | `00b052f0` |
| `00b04dc0` | guid, i32 | `00b05360` |
| `00b04e70` | i32, i32 | `00b05440` |
| `00b04ee0` | guid, 6 x i32 | `00b054b0` |
| `00b05000` | 3 x i32 | `00b05520` |
| `00b051b0` / `00b05220` | i32 (identical bodies) | `00b053d0` / `00b05590` |

The 168/2 field order recovered in note 092 (rider guid, mount guid, seat, queue position, unknown,
composite effect, float name vertical offset) matches `00b04c80` field for field, and
`PacketMountResponse::PacketMountResponse` (`00b045b0`) initialises exactly those slots; the same holds
for 168/4 (`00b04dc0` vs `00b046a0`). Those two class bindings are therefore `likely`, not `confirmed`:
the reader itself carries no vtable, and the match rests on the constructors. The remaining readers could
not be bound to a class at all (several sub-opcodes have no ctor initialising their slots), so they keep
mechanical names and claim only the `MountBasePacket` family in their `wire` row.

## 4. The client mount store

`00b03cd0` constructs a `SoeUtil::HashListMap<int,MountClientInfo,16,-1>` (RTTI): header at +4..+0x10
(size, head, tail, count), sixteen 4-byte buckets at +0x14. Its element operations are all in this range:

* `00b04350` / `00b04390` - node allocation (vtable slot 1, fallback allocator with element size 0x40,
  alignment 8) and node free (slot 2, `FreeMemory(ptr, 8)`).
* `00b050d0` - insert: allocate, construct with `00b04a10`, link head/tail (`+0x30` next, `+0x34` prev),
  bucket = key & 0xf from the key at `+0x38` (chain link `+0x3c`) and bump the count.
* `00b04b70` - remove (splice bucket chain, unlink list, destroy node with `00b04830`, free through slot 2).
* `00b05260` - remove-all (drain loop over the head at +8).
* `00b04980` / `00b04a10` / `00b04830` / `00b04a90` - record init (id -1 at +0, `SoeUtil::IString<char>`
  at +0x10 with the shared empty buffer, invalid guid at +0x20/+0x24, three flag bytes at +0x28..+0x2a),
  key store at +0x38, IString member release (locks the shared buffer refcount at buffer-4 and calls the
  string vtable slot 2), and the scalar deleting destructor.

The automount chain becomes visible across functions: `00b03c90` (`SetAutoMountId`, logs to
`ClientMountLog.txt`) writes the id to client+0x64; the predicate `00b047b0` (called only by the
`Mountable npc ready, automount %d, guid %I64u` handler `0096de80`) matches guid **and** that id against a
list entry; `00b047f0` maps a guid back to the entry id (or 0xffffffff).

Two siblings `00b03d40`/`00b03dd0` (`engine.refptr`) assign reference-counted members of the mount client
object `00b05cb0`, and `00b043d0`/`00b04420` release the same members from EH unwind funclets.
`00b04310` (refresh pair, called from the `Mount` and `Dismount` paths `00972d30`/`00973050`) and
`00b042e0` (flag byte at `[this+0x70]+0x2b1`, caller `00bff440`) are the rider/mount visual refresh hooks.
`00b04290` is the logged send path (`SendMountRequest: target %I64u`) that forwards through virtual slot
0xdc; its caller `0096de80` is the mountable-NPC handler.

## 5. Server-contract inferences (hypotheses, not recovered server code)

* 168/6 and 168/8 are client->server requests (mount number, and item definition id + boolean). The
  original server had to resolve them against the character's owned mount rows before answering with
  168/2; the client's own ownership view is the `HashListMap<int,MountClientInfo>` filled from 168/5.
* 168/2 and 168/4 are server->client replies; their bodies decode into the client without further state.
* 168/9 (SetAutomount), 10/11/13 (trial) and 15 (upgrade response) are inferred **client-received**: the
  00b06100 handler logs `MountHandler:StartTrial`, `MountHandler:EndTrial`, `UpgradeSuccessful` and
  `Automount after upgrade: id %d`, and `SetAutoMountId` is a sink that only stores. No emulator handler
  exists for these five sub-opcodes, so the direction rests on that handler evidence.
* The header layout (u16 opcode + u8 sub-opcode inside the mount payload) constrains any re-implementation
  of the tunnel: a serializer that emits only a single sub-opcode byte will not match this reader.

## 6. Contradictions and gaps

* Subsystem bookkeeping is inconsistent in accepted records: `0089f9c0` (the `MountBasePacket` family
  constructor) is `net.packet.vehicle`, while `009aa410` (`PacketDismountRequest`, same family) is
  `net.mount`. This shard uses `net.mount` for the family; the earlier record may need retagging.
* Note 092 lists sub-opcodes 1..15 with 14 = `PacketMountUpgradeRequest`, 12 unused. This range contains
  no constructor for 1 or 14, so the request half of the upgrade pair is still uncovered.
* `00b036b0` shows that this controller hierarchy places the scalar deleting destructor in vtable slot 7;
  reviewers should not expect slot 0 (here it is a type-id getter).
* `00b048a0` is a second call site of the record destructor `00b04830` with no caller and no vtable slot.
* `00b04e70`, `00b04ee0`, `00b05000`, `00b051b0`, `00b05220` remain class-unbound: their sub-opcodes are
  not determined by in-function evidence, and no constructor in this range initialises their exact slots.
* `00b042e0`'s byte at +0x2b1 and the three record flag bytes at +0x28..+0x2a have no recovered meaning.
