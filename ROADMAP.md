# What's still being worked on

Free Realms Expanded can already run a good part of the original game against our own server, but a lot is unfinished. This page is the honest list. For each area it says what works today, what is missing and why, and how an outside contributor could help. For what the current build does, see [What's new in the README](README.md#whats-new).

The areas are ordered by roughly how many players each one touches. The first ones matter to everybody who logs in. The last ones matter to a smaller group.

**Status words.** "Seen in the real client" means someone ran the original Windows client against our server and watched it work. "Tests only" means automated tests or our scripted test player exercised it, but nobody has watched it in the real client yet. A rule marked **(R)** is a reconstruction: the original server's code does not survive, so we made the rule up from evidence and kept it easy to change.

**How you can help.** The most valuable thing you can give us is evidence of how the original game behaved, because no original server code or data survives. That means memories, screenshots, gameplay videos, spreadsheets of prices and drop rates, and recordings of the original servers' network traffic if you ever captured any. Open an issue on the project's GitHub page and describe what you remember; a vague memory with a date is still useful, and we mark anything we are unsure of. Please do not send game files (client programs, asset packs or extracted art), anything you do not have the right to share, or other players' private details such as account names or private chat. Data checks and code are welcome too. A licence has not been chosen yet, so open an issue to talk before sending a large code contribution.

## Known issues from the latest real-client test

On 26 October 2026 the original Windows client was run twice against the newest build. Everything below was seen on screen. We understand most of these already, and the area sections further down explain how they fit in.

1. **Bought or granted pets do not appear.** Buying a pet at a shop, or being handed one with an admin command, reaches the client but the pet shows in neither the bag nor the Pets window. The bag hides pet items by design, and the Pets window only reads a separate pet roster message that the server does not send at purchase time. Until it does, nobody can summon a new pet to test following or care. See [Pets](#4-pets).
2. **Item pictures are missing in some shops.** The Crab at one pet shop and a pet in the Station Cash shop have no picture, and the magnifier button on the Crab does nothing. The client's own log lists many image ids it could not find, so vendor pictures are likely missing more broadly. See [Shops](#3-shops-items-and-the-station-cash-marketplace).
3. **The Marketplace "Sort By" menu says "undefined", and Hot Items is empty.** The same word shows in the Popular Items box on the welcome splash. In the Boombox category, names, pictures, descriptions and prices now display correctly, so this is the remaining rough edge.
4. **Town greeters leave out their explanation.** Snowpetal and other greeters say their lines but not the text that explains the town's game or activity.
5. **Several NPC shops open empty.** Mr. Twinkle's shop is one example. They need their stock filled in. The shop menu itself works.
6. **Many NPCs still have no dialog.** Clicking them opens a conversation with nothing to say.
7. **Seasonal event NPCs are always there.** They should appear only while their event runs. The Halloween dance battle already follows its schedule correctly. The others do not.
8. **Fishing does not get going.** After you arrive at a fishing area, the minigame does not start, and the fish finder shows no list of fish.
9. **Warpstones do not spin above their altars, and the Warp Watcher NPCs do nothing when you click them.**
10. **The balloon-animal pet is sold in a shop but should not be.** It was a seasonal event reward, so we plan to take it out of the shop and give it to an event instead.

Things that did pass in the same test: the Boombox Marketplace category now shows names, icons and real prices, the second world radio plays in the right place, and mounting and dismounting no longer flashes the character.

## 1. Joining a server and playing together

**What works today.** The server runs on one machine and the original client connects to it over the local network. Several players can share a server: they see each other's looks, outfits, jobs and mounts, chat, form groups, trade, and inspect each other, and a moderator can mute, kick or ban. In the open world, players in the same area see the same enemies, with shared health and kills. Most of this has been checked with scripted test players rather than a crowd of real ones. Login uses key pairs instead of passwords, so a server never holds anything a player could reuse elsewhere. Our scripted test players have put the server through ten-player stress runs without errors, and one has logged in from outside the host machine over a private network address. A real client has also run next to a scripted player and watched it appear, walk and chat. Some of this is covered only by tests: guild and matchmaking requests are routed and tested, but no real player has tried them.

**What is missing, and why.** There is no public server yet, and no test with two real clients on two PCs. By design the servers refuse public internet addresses. The old game protocol runs over UDP with little protection of its own, so the security notes keep the open internet closed and have remote friends join over an encrypted private network instead. A design exists for an encrypted tunnel built into the launcher plus a small forwarding process (see [Public Transport](notes/Design/Public%20Transport.md) and the [Security Model](notes/Design/Security%20Model.md)), but none of it is built. The limits (16 players in total, 4 per network address, one server process, one JSON save file per character and no database) are tuned for a playtest of about ten people, not for hundreds.

Group fights are the clearest gap. When a grouped player starts a battle, the other members get the original invite and enter the same instance, but each member still fights their own copy of the enemies, so two players cannot yet fight the same summoner in an arena. Open-world enemies are shared. Sharing an arena needs one simulation per arena instance and a way to hand every member the result, which is designed but not built.

The biggest practical blocker for outside testers is setup. The server needs a startup snapshot and several reference packages that are prepared from the original client and from captured game traffic, and none of that is in this repository. Until a setup can build them from your own copy of the client, only the maintainers can run a realm. Two smaller bugs also sit here: a brand-new character's client still tries to select a quest that belonged to the character we modelled it on, and inside the world the client's membership flag is not set correctly for members, so a few member-only prompts can show wrongly.

**How you can help.** A security review of the login, handshake and packet-handling code against the written Security Model is very welcome: try to break it. If you have two PCs and the original client, tell us in an issue, because you would be an early tester once setup is reproducible. Memories help with group behaviour: how big groups could be, what the invite and the group window looked like, and what guilds and matchmaking offered. Advice from anyone who has run a community game server on the public internet would shape the hosting plan.

## 2. Quests, dialog and the living world

**What works today.** The quest engine handles offers, accepting, the tracker, turn-in, abandoning, quest markers over NPCs and the "Take Me There" pointer. Objectives can be talking to someone, killing, collecting, gathering, crafting, visiting a place, finishing a minigame or dungeon, or reaching a job level. About 94 of the 678 quest records in our tables are switched on. They cover the job lines (for example Warrior, Wizard, Medic, Archer, Brawler, Ninja, Miner and Chef) and a few regions (Merry Vale, Sanctuary, Seaside, the Wilds, Wugachug and Briarwood). Offer, accept, tracking and talk objectives have been seen in the real client. The NPC tables list about 1,200 regional NPCs, plus about 2,800 extra placements of townsfolk, critters and props. NPCs tied to an enabled quest greet you with a line recovered from the client's own text.

**What is missing, and why.** Most quests are still switched off, because a quest only works when every one of its objectives can run. The research behind this covers 603 quests from the game's final form, with about 1,260 objectives between them. At the last planning pass (25 October), the objectives that could not run were held back for these reasons: about 244 have no evidence yet about what the objective actually is; about 268 name an NPC whose position we have not placed; about 138 need the server to judge a rule (use an item, click a world object, an outcome from a minigame); about 94 need a clickable object or NPC that is not in the world yet; about 66 need an enemy definition; and about 19 need an NPC row. A few quests are tied to one specific missing feature. All 63 Card Duelist quests wait for the card game. All 26 Pet Trainer quests wait for pets to work end to end. A Medic minigame quest and two package-recovery quests need their minigames to report a result. One quest needs a boss fight that a quest can start, one needs a way to spend coins as a goal, and three have givers we have not identified. Even when a batch looked ready, the checks kept it honest: one recent batch of 20 Sanctuary quests turned out to have only 6 that run end to end, so the other 14 stayed switched off with a note on why.

Dialog is thin. Only about 175 of the 1,209 regional NPC rows have a recovered greeting line, so most NPCs have nothing to say. The server also holds just one line per talk step, so quests with several lines, player answers and quiz retries cannot be played yet. About 480 NPC rows only have a guessed position (some have since been drawn from community spawn lists), so those NPCs are not in the world. The Job Browser's Start button works only for the jobs whose "Become a" quest we have written. Town greeters need their explainer text, as in the known issues above.

**How you can help.** If you remember what an NPC said, who stood where, or what a quest asked you to do and what it paid, write it down. Exact wording matters most: dialog lines, quiz questions and answers, and reward amounts. Gameplay videos of quest lines are gold, particularly of the Card Duelist, Pet Trainer, Fisherman, Archer and Soccer Star lines, which have the fewest quests switched on (none of 63 Card Duelist quests and none of 26 Pet Trainer quests, for example). We also need the names of the three unidentified quest givers. If you like data work, spot-check NPC positions in `prototype/osfr-tools/data/content/npcs` against your memory of the map and tell us which ones are wrong.

## 3. Shops, items and the Station Cash Marketplace

**What works today.** Vendors open a shop window with coin prices, buying and selling work, and selling back pays one tenth of the buy price, the rule the original tooltips showed. Our tables hold shop entries for about 17,000 items. Station Cash is earned by finishing races, matches, dungeons and quests (the amounts are our guess and easy to change), and the Marketplace spends it. It sells rides in sub-categories, boomboxes and house lots, and it refuses a second copy of a ride you already own. The Boombox category was seen in the real client with correct names, pictures, descriptions and prices.

**What is missing, and why.** About 194 vendor definitions exist, but only about 100 of them have stock lists, so many shops are empty. Mr. Twinkle's is one. Many item pictures are missing, and the Marketplace's Sort By menu and Hot Items category are broken (see the known issues). Pets are not listed in the Marketplace at all yet, even though we hold about 84 Station Cash price rows for them from the wiki. This is blocked on the pet-purchase fix described under Pets. We also hold a list of roughly 7,200 items whose source is known (a shop, a drop, a prize wheel or a collection). Turning that into shop and drop data is planned, after the work on zone ids described under Travel, starting with vendors and the coin shop. Prices and drop rates we cannot find are marked (R), and some Station Cash prices are our own scaling of the wiki's numbers.

**How you can help.** Shop contents are the most useful memory: what an NPC vendor sold, roughly what it cost, and whether it was members-only. Screenshots or videos of a shop window are better still. For the Marketplace, tell us what the categories, the sort options and the Hot Items shelf listed, and which items cost what in Station Cash. For icons, tell us when an item showed a picture in the original and what it looked like.

## 4. Pets

**What works today.** Every pet and Pet Pal item in the client's data can be adopted (113 pet types, each with its model, vanity flag and trick list). Pets follow their owner, snap to the ground, catch up or warp when left behind, and dance beside an owner who is dancing. The care panel takes feeding, watering and grooming items, and the Pet Browser record carries each pet's icon, name, tint, tricks and clothing classes. All of this is tests only, because nobody can get a pet to appear in the real client yet.

**What is missing, and why.** The first fix is the one in the known issues. In the original game pets were not bag items. The server created the pet when you bought or received it and sent the pet roster. Our server adds the item but never sends the roster, so the Pets window stays empty. The fix is to adopt the pet in the same step as the purchase or grant, and to refuse the purchase before any coins move if the roster is full. After that, a long list is waiting: listing pets in the Marketplace and at the coin merchants that really sold them, keeping the summoned pet across warps, dungeons and minigames, grooming and petting, tricks and favourites, pet clothing and attachments, idle animations and thought bubbles, the speed boost a happy pet gives its owner, and the treasure chests a happy pet digs up.

Pet movement has its own blocker. The server has no collision data, so a pet walks straight through fences, walls and bridges, and "Take Me There" does the same. Collision has to be built from the placed-object meshes in the client's map files, and then a walk grid and routes on top of it. Almost every pet feature is built from the client's own code, with community projects as references, because we have no recordings of the original server's pet messages. The numbers for needs, happiness and treasure odds are reconstructed. The balloon-animal pet needs an event to belong to.

**How you can help.** Pets are where outside memory helps most. We would like to know which tricks each pet knew and what the animations looked like; how fast needs fell and what happiness did; what petting and grooming felt like and how long they took; what the pet's thought bubbles said; what the treasure chests contained and how often they appeared; and how the balloon-animal pet was originally obtained. Packet captures of pet traffic from the original servers would be the single most valuable thing anyone could send.

## 5. Combat, jobs and dungeons

**What works today.** All six combat jobs have basic attacks, level-locked abilities and equipped-weapon checks. Combat numbers live in one data table that can be edited while the server runs. They are fitted to the final-era figures on the wiki and to reviewed gameplay video. Open-world enemy camps cover levels 1 to 20, and eleven field battles run end to end, including Bixie Hive, Sheep Watch, Bandit Hideout and King of the Ring. A grouped player's battle invite reaches the rest of the group, and the paid revive works. In the real client we have seen *Troll Summoner Madness!* from start to finish, Medic abilities, the level-1 health value and power-up pickups. The others are tests only.

**What is missing, and why.** Dungeons are the big gap. We have descriptions of 75 dungeons and the client's map markers for 41 of them, but only the opening cave runs in its own scene. The eleven field battles run in one shared arena scene instead. The client has no dungeon manager, so the server owns everything: the scene, the zone id, the enemy list, the goals, the rewards and the prize wheel. The notes in [Dungeon Instances](notes/Research/Dungeon%20Instances.md) give the smallest path to a real one, starting with Misty Mountain, whose scene files are all present in the client. Several things hold the rest back. Enemy models for many dungeon enemies are not tied to enemy definitions yet. Some scenes, such as Vale of the Ancients, are not in the files we could extract. Above all, the prize wheel's contents are decided by the server, so the client data tells us the prize names but never which prizes each dungeon offered or the odds. Group fights, as above, are still per player.

Balance is the other open area. Every combat number is a fit to limited evidence, and some ability unlock levels come from old class guides and may be off. Enemy health comes from counting hits in video, so it is only as good as the clips we found.

**How you can help.** Videos are the best evidence here. A fight with visible damage numbers and enemy health bars lets us fit health and damage. Videos of dungeon prize wheels (or lists of what you won, and where) would unblock the rewards. Ability unlock levels per job, what each ability did and the tier names are all useful, and so are dungeon walkthroughs: enemy names and counts, boss mechanics, and goals.

## 6. Travel, regions and the map

**What works today.** In the real client, the Atlas lists the warpstones you have attuned to, you attune by walking up to one, travel works, Teleport to Safety works, and your attunements survive a relog. A character saved off the map is put back at the nearest warpstone when it logs in, and there is an admin command to move a stuck character. A shared table now holds the client's ten regions and named places with a "which region is this position in" lookup. That lookup is tests only. The client's "Take Me There" request is answered with a straight-line path, which was found not to work at first and has since been fixed in tests; it has not been rechecked in the real client.

**What is missing, and why.** The client zones by scene name, and our own tables use region numbers we made up. Giving every NPC, place and dungeon a real zone and region is the groundwork for several things, including the "Now entering" banner, teleports that land in the right place and dungeon scenes. This groundwork has begun, but most of the data pass is still ahead. The warpstone problem in the known issues (no spinning, Warp Watchers silent) is an animation and interaction gap we have not filled yet. "Take Me There" and anything that chases or leads cannot go around obstacles until the client's road graph and collision data are loaded, as with pets. The game's own marker icons for warpstones, ore veins and farm plots on the minimap are also not sent. The game's sky and clock follow a recovered time-sync reply, but nobody has looked at them in the real client yet.

**How you can help.** If you remember where the "Now entering" banner appeared, the exact wording, or where one region ended and the next began, tell us. We also want the list of warpstones and where they stood, plus how Warp Watchers behaved when clicked. Screenshots of the Atlas at different stages would help check what is on the map.

## 7. Fishing and the other minigames

**What works today.** Kart races start from driver NPCs at the portal areas. In the real client the NPC menu and loading work, and an earlier three-lap race paid coins and XP once. The other minigames are tests only: demolition derby, Soccer Star with a trainer and a Snowhill match, chess with rewards only for properly played games, mining and harvesting nodes you click in the world, and sparkling collection nodes. Fishing guides take you to the fishing hole's own scene, and the client's own fishing settings are sent.

**What is missing, and why.** Fishing is the worst of these. It does not start in the real client (see the known issues), and its logic has a bigger flaw: following the on-screen prompt to tug when a fish nibbles currently makes the fish escape, so a player who plays it as designed always loses. Farm plots are not yet an entrance to the regional Harvesting games, and Snowhill's copper veins are not placed. The Games window only lists the activities we run, while the client's own activity table has 417 rows, so most entries do not appear. The daily "Spin For The Win!" wheel is missing entirely: the client has the wheel movie, but nothing offers it at world entry or answers its messages. Quitting a kart race midway had no exit the client accepted, and a fix has not been rechecked in the real client. Battle Mages, ship combat and Treasure War are researched but not built.

**How you can help.** For fishing we need the fish tables: which fish lived where, how rare they were, and what the fish finder listed. For the daily wheel, tell us the prizes and how often they came up. Soccer, derby and chess memories (rules, scores, rewards, difficulty) help calibrate rewards, which are all (R) today. Videos of any of these minigames would let us check the flow against the original.

## 8. Seasonal events

**What works today.** An event system lets NPCs spawn, animate, speak, broadcast and leave on a timeline and a wall-clock schedule, gated by season. It runs Bruce's concert, which alternates between the Snowhill and Wugachug stages every two hours, and the Vampires vs Werewolves dance-off in Blackspore, every 30 minutes in season. The dance-off has been seen in the real client. Season windows exist for events such as Snow Days, the Festival of Hearts and the birthday celebration, and Halloween decorations only show in season. An admin can open and close the scavenger hunt, and can start any event by command. Some seasonal rides are written but switched off.

**What is missing, and why.** Seasonal NPCs are not tied to their events (see the known issues), so they stand around all year. The tagging and spawn gate exist for the dance-off and need extending to everything else. Players cannot join the dance-off: the original had costume teams, a voting box and a cake only participants could use, and ours is a show you watch. Whole sets of seasonal items from the original are not built yet, including placed birthday cakes and a Mystery Cake that hands out one-minute surprises, silly-string cans, and snowball piles in Snowhill. Snowballs and thrown items need a projectile message layout that we have not recovered from the client yet. Each event is also hand-choreographed, so each one needs its own evidence for the timeline, speech and prizes.

**How you can help.** Event memories are very valuable: what time it ran, who spoke and what they said, what you could do, and what you won. Tell us which events ran in which season, and what the balloon-animal pet's event was. Videos of any event are better still.

## 9. Housing

**What works today.** In the real client, every new character owns the Wilds Condo starter lot, My Houses lists it, you can enter and leave, and Click to Decorate opens with a drawn preview of the item you chose. The rest is tests only: placing and picking up fixtures that persist across relogs, other players visiting your house, buying lot deeds in the Marketplace, and a table of 1,186 placeable fixtures. 23 of 31 lot definitions are switched on.

**What is missing, and why.** The last real-client test could not place furniture: the client refused our placements in the yard, and no placement message reached the server, so the client's own rule about where a fixture may stand still needs working out. Eight lots stay off: we cannot yet match them to a scene, or (for two) they turned out to be farm plots rather than houses. The house directory (publishing a house, rating it, browsing the popular, new and featured lists, searching) answers with an empty list, and the "Visiting the Neighbors" quest depends on it. House customization, upkeep and farm lots are not built.

**How you can help.** If you decorated houses, you know what the decorating bar showed, which areas allowed which items, what the item limits were, and what the directory looked like. Packet captures of decorating would settle the placement question quickly. Screenshots of the directory lists and the rating panel help, as does any memory of which lots existed in which regions.

## 10. The Card Duelist card game

**What works today.** The original card game ran as a separate online service reached from the client's own card module. The server now speaks the service's login and gateway protocol (worked out from the client's module) and checks one-time launch tickets. The card-game entries appear in the Games window, and two Card Duelist quests are bound to their original records. All of this is tests only.

**What is missing, and why.** The game itself is missing: no deck, no rules, no duel. The client's card module tells us how to log in and how its messages are framed, but the rules and the duel messages are not worked out yet. Treasure War is a different card client with its own service and is further off. About 124 duel entries and the whole Card Duelist quest line, all 63 quests, wait for this. There is a detailed design in [Card Game](notes/Research/Card%20Game.md).

**How you can help.** Anything about the card game rules would unblock design. That means the full card list with stats and effects, starter decks and the tutorial, how a turn works and how a duel is won, how cards were won or bought, and what the Card Duelist job's duels offered. Screen recordings of duels are very helpful. A packet capture of the card service from the original servers would be the single most valuable item.

## 11. Tools for builders and moderators

**What works today.** The Studio and World Builder let trusted builders place NPCs and exploration coins in the running world, start and stop events, and see their changes saved. Roles (player, builder, admin, moderator) decide who may run which commands, and moderators can mute, kick and ban.

**What is missing, and why.** Quest authoring in the game, a review step that promotes finished content into the main world, authoring of new item definitions, and live editing of collection nodes are all still to come. A public sandbox, where the community builds in a shared world, is the long-term aim. It depends on public hosting and on those roles and review steps being solid first.

**How you can help.** Try the Studio and tell us what is confusing. Ideas for content you would build, and what controls you would want, shape the design. Moderation memories help too: what chat rules and limits the original game had.

## Further out

These are not scheduled, but they are on the list: a database for saves, public hosting, a replacement client written in Rust, and the remaining dungeons, guild features and minigames.
