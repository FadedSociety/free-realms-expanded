## 1. Effect-node update (00c68550, 00c68b90)

`00c68b90` is a timer-throttled motion update for one effect node: it compares a 64-bit time
(`FUN_007027f0`) against `this+0x70/+0x74`, re-rolls its next deadline at `+0x78`
(`FUN_0071acb0` between `+0x1c` and `+0x20` times the 1000.0 constant at `DAT_017f0250`), picks a
yaw (random delta from `FUN_00738380`, or `fpatan` of a target position when the flags at
`+0x24..+0x27` select that branch), wraps it with the 2*pi constant at `DAT_017eb268`
(6.2831855, read from the image) and steps the position at `+0x60` along the facing vector.
`00c68550` then composes the 4x4 transform (quaternion at `+0x50`, scale/offset dwords at
`+0x40/+0x44/+0x48`) and calls virtual slot `+0x20` on every child returned by
`FUN_008c52f0`/`FUN_008c5330`.

Its caller `00ced210` is `ClientAreaRandomEffectProperty::vftable[7]`, which pins the domain:
this is the per-frame behaviour of a **client-area random effect** (`nmq show 00ced210`). The
second caller, `009f7d10`, runs the same step from the placement path. Two small SoeUtil fixed
string constructors sit at the head of the shard (`00c68f30` `IStringFixed<char,10>`, `00c68f90`
`StringFixed<10>`, both vtable-confirmed) and `00c68ff0` parses one definition row from a token
stream (`FUN_0072d900`/`FUN_006fc080`/`FUN_0072d4e0`, `_strtoul` base 16) into a packed
`id | tint<<24` word at `+0x14` plus a float at `+0x18`, clamping the tint with the 1.0 constant
at `DAT_0175b420` and the 255.0 constant at `DAT_017ed9d0`.

## 2. GUI data sources: claim window and marketing (00c69140..00c6a720)

RTTI names the classes: `ClaimDataSource::InternalDsBase`, `ClaimDataSource::AvailableItems`,
`ClaimDataSource::ClaimItemContents`, `PromotionalDataSource::LastPromotionalAward` and
`PromotionalBundleDatasource`. They all derive from the GUI data-table base
(`FUN_00922890` runs `GuiDsTable`/`GuiUserDataTable` constructors), so they belong to the
existing `engine.gds` subsystem. Each class exposes the same virtual layout:

- slot 1 = field-name table (`00c69150`, `00c691c0`, `00c69c60`, `00c69cd0`),
- slot 2 = row count (`00c69410`, `00c69970`, `00c6a520`, `00c6a5d0`),
- slot 4 = field reader (`00c695a0`, `00c696c0`, `00c6a180`, `00c6a2d0`), which writes into the
  binding through `FUN_009969b0` (integer) / `FUN_008ed320` (string) and resolves string-table
  ids with `FUN_0083d110`.

Field vocabularies recovered from the slot-1 tables: **available items** `ItemId, StackCount,
ImageSetId, ImageTintValue, NameId, DescriptionId`; **claim item contents** `ItemId, ItemName,
ItemQuantity, ItemImageSetId, ItemTint`; **last promotional award** `ItemId, Type, Name,
Description, ImageSetId, TintValue`; **promotional bundle** `Name, Description, ImageSetId,
TintValue, ItemListDetails, BundleId, ItemId, Quantity`. `00c69f90` builds the bundle's
`ItemListDetails` as one wide string from the item list at `+0x20` (formats `%s%s%d%s` /
`%s%s%s%s`, item name resolved through the client singleton `FUN_00769710`), and `00c6a2d0`
serves it for field index 4.

Ownership: `00c69af0` allocates and registers both claim sources under
`BaseClient.ClaimSystem.AvailableItems` and `BaseClient.ClaimSystem.ClaimItemContents`;
`00c6a720` registers `BaseClient.Marketing.LastPromotionalAward`. The two owning members are
stored through byte-identical ref-counted assignment helpers (`00c69240`, `00c692d0`, `00c69d70`,
subsystem `engine.refptr`), and the matching release helpers (`00c69450`, `00c694a0`, `00c694d0`,
`00c69e90`, `00c69ed0`) are cited by EH unwind funclets, i.e. destructor-time cleanups. Bundle
lookups use a 64-bucket hash chain (`00c69e10` lookup, `00c6a270` removal, buckets at `+0x208`
with the chain at node `+0x130`).

The claim sources embed a `ClaimItem` member (destroyed by `FUN_01060db0`) and an item-instance
member (`FUN_0103dd90`), which is the client-side counterpart of the claim/reward flow that the
server drives from elsewhere; nothing in this range sends or parses a packet for it.

**Wire shape.** Header = 2-byte family (byte-read as `s16`, the low two bytes of the dword the
family constructor wrote) + 1-byte sub-opcode: `00c6beb0` reads it (`movsx` of a word at

returns success only when the overflow flag stayed clear and the cursor reached the end (unless
the strictness argument is set). Client payloads, in field order: InviteReply `u32 session, u8
accepted`; Lock `u32 session, u32 revision, u8 locked`; Accept `u32 session, u32 revision`;
Cancel `u32 session`; OfferItem `u32 session, u32 item, u32 resulting count`; CoinCount `u32
session, u32 coins`. Server payloads: invite `string name, u32, u32, u32 session`
(`00c6cab0`), start-session `u32, u32, string name, u32 session` (`00c6cb40`), coin update
`u32 session, u32, u8, u32` (`00c6bf50`), item count `u32 session, u32, u8, u32, u32`
(`00c6c000`), add item `u32 session, u32, u8` + item blob (`00c6ce30` calls `FUN_008fa4b0`),
lock `u32 session, u8` (`00c6c110`), item removed `u32, u32, u32` (`00c6c180`), confirm/cancel/
end-session `u32 session` (`00c6c5f0`, `00c6c530`), message `u8 status` (`00c6c630`), rejected
`string reason` (`00c6cdb0` via `FUN_008e7290`, the length-prefixed string reader).

for the sub-opcode, calls the matching wrapper, compares the parsed session id against its own
`+4`, and raises the UI events `TradeHandler:Init`, `TradeHandler:Destroy`, `TradeCompleted`,
`TradeCanceled`, `TradeCanceledPartnerDisconnected`, `TradeCanceledPartnerOfferInvalid`,
`TradeCanceledYourOfferInvalid`, `TradeCanceledYouEnteredInstancedZone`,
`TradeCanceledPartnerEnteredInstancedZone`, `TradeCanceledYourOfferExceedsPartnersMaxStack`,
`TradeCanceledPartnersOfferExceedsYourMaxStack`, `TradeRejected`, `TradeConfirmation:Hide` and
`TradeConfirmation:StartTradeTimer`. The status byte of sub-opcode 15 is the switch key there:
1 canceled, 2 partner disconnected, 3 partner offer invalid, 4 your offer invalid, 6 completed,
7 you entered an instanced zone, 8 partner entered an instanced zone, 9 your offer exceeds the
partner's max stack, 10 partner's offer exceeds your max stack. That mapping is recovered from
the dispatcher's own arms, not from a capture.

**Send path.** The six client requests are built by the tracker methods `00c6db40`/`00c6dbe0`
(invite reply, accept or refuse), `00c6dc70` (lock), `00c6dcf0` (accept), `00c6dd70` (cancel),
`00c6ddf0` (offer item, resolving the item through `FUN_00cf6c90`) and `00c6de90` (coin count,
called from the UI at `00c05e20`). Each hands the packet to a gateway path
(`00c6d420`, `00c6d530`, `00c6d640`, `00c6d750`, `00c6d860`, `00c6d970`) that opens an allocator
scope (`FUN_008d4a40(0x10000000)`), serialises the body into a ~0x2024-byte stack writer, builds
a tunneled packet (`FUN_0089ae70` world-tunneled for invite-reply/lock/accept/cancel, or
`FUN_0089adc0` plain-tunneled for offer/coin), installs `ClientGateway::BasePacket::vftable` and
submits with `FUN_00894560`. The tracker object itself is built by `00c6c470` (session id `-1` at
`+4`, the global default pair `DAT_01bee968`/`DAT_01bee96c` at `+0x10/+0x14`, an SoeUtil string at
`+0x18`, a 0x18-byte partner helper at `+0xa0` built by `FUN_00cf68d0`, whose literals are
`BaseClient.Trade.PartnerName` / `BaseClient.Trade.PartnerCoinCount`) and torn down by
`00c6be30`.

**Server-contract hypotheses (only these carry a wire link).** The client sends only a session
id plus one payload value per request and never validates another player's stack totals itself:
the original server must have owned the session id, the offer revision, the lock state, the
merged stack counts and the whole-or-nothing transfer, then echoed each change back as
sub-opcodes 11..19. The `TradeMessagePacket` cancellations the client understands mention
exceeding a partner's max stack in either direction, which only the server could evaluate.
Disconnect handling is invisible in this shard: the client reacts to status 2 but nothing here
sends or detects a disconnect.

## Open questions

1. The trade tracker object (`00c6c470` ctor, `00c6be30` dtor) has no RTTI: its binding to the
   dispatcher `00c6df10` rests on the identical field offsets and on `FUN_00cf68d0`'s
   `BaseClient.Trade.*` literals. A reviewer should confirm it is the object `00a1de60`/
   `00a1d6d0` embed rather than a second copy.
2. Item wire shape: `FUN_008fa4b0` (add item) and `FUN_0101cd40`/`FUN_0103dc60` (the member at
   `+0x18` of the item packets) are unlabelled, so the item id/instance-id width in sub-opcodes 9,
   12 and 13 is not fully pinned.
3. `TradeMessagePacket`'s status byte is written as a dword by its constructor but read as one
   byte by `00c6c630`; the true field width is unsettled.
4. The trailing dword `+0x18` of the coin-count update (`00c6bf50`) is read but never used by the
   dispatcher, and the two identity dwords of the invite (`+0x98/+0x9c`) are never named.
5. `TradeEndSessionPacket` (sub-opcode 5) is receive-only in the client (its ctor is called only
   from the dispatcher); a client-initiated session end goes through Cancel (sub-opcode 7)
   instead, which the original protocol may not have required.
6. The `marketing` and `claim` data sources are read-only here: nothing in this range shows who
   populates their tables (the `+0x1c4` table pointer and the row arrays), so their content is a
   separate shard's problem.
7. `00c69360`, `00c69380` and `00c6b5b0..00c6b610` are mechanical wrappers/teardown helpers whose
   classes could not be bound; they are `likely` labels with the virtual slots left unnamed.

## Naming and subsystem decisions to reconcile at the lead level

- `net.trade` is a **new** subsystem node, chosen to match the existing per-family nodes
  (`net.quest`, `net.pet`, `net.coinstore`) because no existing name covers the trade family;
  `net.packet` remains the fallback if the lead prefers fewer nodes.
- `engine.effect` was chosen for `00c68550`/`00c68b90` because their caller `00ced210` is a
  `ClientAreaRandomEffectProperty` virtual; `engine.area` would also fit if the lead treats
  client-area properties as area code.
- Trivial destructor bodies keep the map's existing `BasePacketDtor_<addr>` convention instead of
  asserting a packet class that the body does not prove; the class relationship (which ctor frame
  the unwind funclets belong to) is stated in `purpose` only.
