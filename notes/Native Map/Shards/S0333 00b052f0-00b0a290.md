## Systems in this range

The shard contains mount packet/client handling (00b052f0–00b06db0), audio service and container support (00b06ec0–00b099d0), and client area-shape data (00b09d90–00b0a290). Most intervening functions are allocator, list, reference-count, or ordered-tree helpers. RTTI evidence ties the relevant container methods to `HashListMap<int,MountClientInfo,16,-1>`, `PacketMountList`, `SoundPlayer`, and typed `ClientAreaDataShaped` classes.

## Mount packet flow

00b06100 dispatches mount-family sub-opcodes 2, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11, 13, and 15. It parses response/list bodies, updates client mount/trial/upgrade state, and emits local notifications. The body readers at 00b052f0–00b05590 apply reader-status and bounds checks before accepting decoded fields. 00b056a0 tracks elapsed trial time and emits `MountHandler:TrialTime` at changed, positive whole-minute boundaries. 00b05b80 and 00b05bf0 construct/destroy the `PacketMountList` parser object; 00b05e00 decodes its list body. 00b06cc0 sends `PacketDismountRequest`; 00b06db0 sends item-based mount spawn. Serializer helpers at 00b05a80/00b05ea0 and 00b05ae0/00b05fb0 are linked to their corresponding mount-spawn packet paths through callers.

**Wire hypothesis:** the reconstructed packet catalogue and the client switch together suggest that mount-family responses carry rider/mount identity, queue or result fields, while client requests encode dismount or spawn intent. This is a client-side protocol hypothesis only; it depends on 00b06100, its per-case readers, packet constructors, and the two sender paths above. It does not establish server authorization, trial rules, or gameplay policy.

## Audio and containers

00b098a0 constructs `SoundPlayer`; 00b09930 cleans its arrays, definition lists, and emitter list; 00b099b0 is its scalar deleting destructor. 00b099d0 processes emitter completion and dispatches the exact local callback `HUD:OnSoundComplete`. The nearby sound controls update, stop, pause, or resume sound/emitter records (00b06f60–00b07070 and 00b07890–00b08070). Their operations are local audio state; no packet flow is present.

The functions from 00b082a0 onward include two analogous ordered-tree implementations. Their successor/predecessor direction follows the child and parent offsets in their bodies; rotations, insertion balancing, removal balancing, and pooled-node operations are separate helpers. The first pool uses 28-byte nodes; the second allocates blocks of 100 nodes. Unclear ownership or field semantics remain unnamed.

## Area-shape data

00b09d90–00b09e10 return the shaped-data subobject at `this + 0x1e0`. Shape-name accessors include the literal `sphere` at 00b09e20 and static pointers at 00b09e30/00b09e40; binary bytes at 0181d270 and 0181d274 decode to `box` and `dome`. 00b09e90 writes the `shape=floor` tag. 00b0a290 reads three keyed values plus `radius` into the shaped data. The shape-specific methods 00b0a100 and 00b0a160/00b0a1a0/00b0a250 validate or draw stored shape bounds through shared helpers.

## Review corrections and remaining limits

Review exposed three repeated labeling errors, corrected in the records: destructor bodies were mislabeled as ordinary virtual methods; adjustor thunks were described as if they contained their jump targets' full behavior; and address-range defaults assigned `world.area` to unrelated sound/container functions. Destructors, scalar deleting destructors, and thunks now have separate names and byte-level descriptions. Shared vtable implementation at 00b09eb0 has no exclusive class assignment. The static dome data is identified from binary bytes, not claimed as a function string.

No address-specific vault note was returned by `nmq notes` for the sampled mount/audio/shape anchors; no contradiction with an existing address-cited note was identified. Packet names and the wire hypothesis rely on third-party emulator catalogue evidence, not original server source. The precise semantics of mount trial/upgrade responses, several audio fields, and tree-node keys remain unresolved.
